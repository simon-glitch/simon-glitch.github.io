Okay, so you can define the inverse of a function like so. f and g are inverses of each other if:
* for every x in the domain of either (you can pick just one domain), f(g(x)) = g(f(x)) = x.

Now, that is fun and all, but what about a half inverse? We'll define a half inverse like so:
* for every x in the domain of f, there is at least one y such that f(y) = x and g(x) = y
* for every x in the domain of g, there is at least one z such that g(z) = x and f(x) = z

This works very well if the domains are both infinite, so that there are lots of y's and z's to spend and distribute on the x's. Imagine a random looking function between from the reals to the reals for example.
