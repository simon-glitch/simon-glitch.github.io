# World building
The name of the world is Marwin.

In Marwin, magic is everything, but most people don't know magic. Most people would be perfectly capable of controlling it, but the church and state have been trying to keep magic under control for ages. The government has 3 parts. The church, the "state", and the mages. Or more accurately, most countries have that structure, whether officially or unofficially. Things vary quite a bit, but the mages branch often doesn't exist in places where less people know magic.

The mages branch is formally known as the mage guild, and on tbe outside, to the uninformed person, it looks similar to the adventurer guild or merchant guilds. However none of those things are similar. Merchant guilds are actual small entities of their own. The adventurer guild is mostly run by local governments and gets a lot of subsidization. Most branches of the adventurer guild don't make a profit.

The mage guild keeps all of its own politics undercover. Only clergy and other church members are generally aware of this.

The church and the mage guild are always bickering with each other, like opposing political parties. Though rules and policies on how they interact vary based on the local government. Where government refers to the effective combination of all three branches.

The mage guild has a subsection called the archmage guild. These are the handful of people, in each local area, that are the most powerful mages. The most powerful member of a local archmage guild is the leader, but each guild has a coverup leader (chosen by descendence, or by vote if there is no reasonable descendent). The public and the vast majority of the "state" don't even know that the coverup leader is a coverup, and they instead believe he is the real leader.

The coverup exists partly to reduce the risk of assassination of the important actual leader, but that purpose is only secondary. The primary purpose is to keep the leader out of as much politics as possible. The mage guild kinda hates politics, since mages are all about knowledge and increasing power through magic.

# Geography
The world is completely flat and seemingly infinite. Though many mages theorize it might be a hypertorus, there is no proof of that. Most mages, and all the ancient texts are certain that the world definitely does not have an edge that can be fallen off of. Because if it did, cats would have knocked everything off the edge by now. This is based on an experiment that put cats on a floating island for a hundred years and they did knock almost everything off the edge. Another reason is that if you could get to the edge of the world, you could go underneath it, and access a potentially infinite space with infinite resources. Or you might find enormous monsters down there. Also, some mages think it would be unstable, while a hypertorus would make the world's natural magic much more stable.

Day, night, the sun, and the moon are all part of an illusion created by the natural magic of the world, which is general knowledge. And there are no stars, but ancient texts do describe additional lights appearing in the sky due to special magic. Also, really powerful magic can change the color and appearance of the sky in the local area.

If you fly up into the air, the natural air mana becomes more and more powerful the father you go from land, and this makes the winds stronger. Because of this, skyscrapers, floating islands, flying creatures, and mages of spells flying high in the air, all often have to expend more mana to fly higher. So usually creatures fly low and things are kept closer the ground. A floating island has its own earth mana and presence, which allows it to float higher the bigger the island is, but there is still a limit to how high it can go. Mountains naturally have earth mana that pushes back against the local air mana, which generally raises the maximum flying altitude by the exact altitude of the mountain.

# The story
I like power fantasies, so that's what I'm going to write. But a power fantasy is fun when you add practical challenges and barriers that the MC has to deal with. Like, the MC running into a problem they can't solve right now, but later on when they get a new skill, they are surprised to find they can. I like it when its indirect too, like the MC switching between different problems and not solving problems directly. I like that since it reflects how life is for me, being nuerodivergent.
