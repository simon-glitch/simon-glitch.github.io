Rather than making an Undertale AU, I'll just make my own Undertale-inspired story. I'll also be giving a whole new emotional feel to its motifs. Because motifs really do change based on context.

For the story, you have isekaid in another world, like in Undertale. The PC is named Rollie, but she has amnesia, plus other problems. There is a ending where you actually help her get her memories back. And like in Deltarune, you are a magical spirit that is possessing her. But unlike in Deltarune, it doesn't bother her.

Like in Undertale, this game has multiple endings. But I am making my endings far more distinct, because that is my favorite kind of game. Specifically, I really like the Stanley Parable. It is the perfect example of distinct and fun endings.

The conflict of the story is that a calamity known as "the darkness" is trying to destroy the world and everything in it. A few years ago, Asriel sacrificed himself to stop the darkness. But all it did was buy time. The outcome of the story depends on the player's actions, right? Well actually no matter what you do, the darkness will be mysteriously defeated, usually due to Simon's involvement. However, the game actively hides this information in neutral endings.

Simon's power is limited by the darkness, which acts as my general reason he doesn't just solve the problem on his own. But the limits are complex, much like a curse, which allows Simon to act as a form of plot armor. This is explained by Simon and hinted at by NPCs as a bit of a joke about plot armor and loopholes in stories.

There is a genocide route, but in it, Simon will of course stop you, after you've killed all life in the world. He'll literally force you to reset. When you fight Simon, there is a song called "The World Survives". Its motif is actually based on me mis-hearing Megalovania, but I plan to use it in othe songs in the game's sound track. Though I will probably make Asriel's motif the primary motif.

Who is Simon? Well Simon is literally my name, and the idea is the character is me but primarily not me. In the lore, he is an extremely powerful slime who has been planning how to defeat the darkness. He is also a master of every kind of magic. Being a slime, he is technically not alive, but this is only used in a joke about you trying to kill him.

If you don't do the genocide route, Simon can be used both as the in-game wiki and as an easy/assist mode.

The particular details of how Simon saves the world change based on which ending you take.

Endings often involve multiple consecutive runs.

In your second run of the genocide route, there is a second calamity called "corruption", which is happening at the same time as the darkness, making the world much more grim. Simon will ask you to cleanse the darkness within you. If you agree, you get the "therapy ending". Elsewise, you continue to the third run. In the third run, you are asked right from the start if you want to choose the path of darkness or the path of light. The path of darkness will invert the colors on the display, and cause light sources to appear black, and shadows to look like light. The path of light doesn't change the display. In both routes, you can still option cleanse the darkness within you, and this does not affect the sub endings for each path. At the end of third run, regardless of which path you chose, you are asked if you will give in to the darkness. This changes the final scene / boss fight. This gives four different sub endings. The third boss fight is always against Asriel, but his persona is different based on your choices. If you choose darkness both times, and don't redeem yourself, Simon will send you to hell. Hell is a whole different world with its own plot and endings.

There is no Flowey, so if you do a pacifist run, nothing special happens immediately. However, the various characters will "teach" you things, unlocking new options for the second run. If you don't befriend all of the characters, the darkness will cause them to stage a coup. You see, in this game, all of the bosses are monsterfolk that are being manipulated by the darkness. Even if you establish peace, the darkness can still convince them all to sacrifice you in order to erase the darkness fully. If this happens, Asriel will explain to you directly that you need to genuinely befriend everyone next time, for your own good, so you can go back "HOME". You then ask what HOME is and he says "U-u-u-uh, n-n-no! I didn't say that. I meant your world!". This hints there is more to him than meets the eye.

The pacifist route also has a sub ending for if you cause as much harm as possible without technically killing anyone. This one is called "maybe a psycho ending".

When you do the pacifist route properly, like in Undertale, Asriel comes back to life. In this game, he is controlled by the darkness and it is making its last attempt at destroying you. Asriel is so powerful that Simon has to help you later in the fight. Once you finally win, Asriel will be back to normal, for a short while. Just long enough to chat. He'll explain that he is actually you brother from your home world. And that he had to sacrifice himself in order to save the monster world, and then ended up reincarnating in your home world. And then he explains that you're actually in a coma in your home world. And that you need to wake up. And he says it as a shortened form of the End Poem from Minecraft. However, when you wake up, you learn that Asriel died saving your life, and that you're lucky the coma was so short. Completing the pacifist route unlocks the hall of memories, which is an in-game visualization of the endings you've seen and also a way to view your stats.

Toriel has been replaced with Birtha. And the ruins is now Birtha's home. She takes care of the monsters, but she has never managed to teach them to be nice to other people.

The name you choose for your PC has several effects.
* For example, choosing names like "Fast" or "Speed" gives you items / abilities that make you go faster.
* Choosing the name skip causes you to automatically skip all dialogue, and plays hillarious "Skip" voice overs that get more exasperated the longer the skipped dialogue was.
* If you choose the name "Darkness", you get the darkness effect from the genocide route, and neutral endings are all changed so they end in your becoming one with the darkness and then being defeated.
* If you name has inappropriate character strings in it, then NPCs will often says your name is "kid-friendly", which is just ironic. If you name is "Irony", characters will say they've never heard of it. Simon will ask "But in WHAT WAY is it ironic? Don't you know there are like 8 types or paradoxes... wait, that's off topic". If you try to name yourself "Paradox", "no name", "null", "NaN", or "undefined", the game will jokingly say "TypeError: Cannot read properties of undefined ("[Your name]" coerced to undefined).
* If you name yourself "Flowey" or "Flowery", the intro will change with Simon throwing friendship pellets at you, which are actual items you can use. If you name yourself a gun, the game will make it so all of your attacks work like a shotgun. That means they are inaccurate, have a high chance of hitting you or allies instead of enemies, and make an incredibly loud noise (that makes you lose your hearing in-game).
* If you choose the name "Dead", you will die during the fall. It will say "You died. I can say for sure that dead is a perfect description of you.". Then the game will just play Fallen Down continuously until you hit the select button.
* If you name yourself Steve, whenever you try to say "Steve", you'll go into a coughing fit. Eventually you'll start telling people you have Autolabelicaphobia. Autolabelicaphobia is the fear of things that describe themselves. Saying your name is a way of descibing yourself. Once you know a character knows what autolabelicaphobia means, you will not be able to tell them that you have it again if they ask just to confirm, because then they would actually know what you mean, which would make Autolabelicaphobia an effective description of you, to them specifically. It's very round-a-bout indeed, which I absolutely love. Now this might be confusing, since your character is named Rollie, but she's lost her memory and the darkness is messing with her head.
* If you name yourself "love", when you level up, the game will say "All this violence fills you with LOVE!". My game directly says "Your level of violence went ups" when you level up for combat level. You also have your social level.
* If you name yourself ASD/ADHD/etc., the game gives you the option to add extra simulation, like videos on the side of the screen, and YouTube / meme style sound effects.
* If you name yourself "hard" the game will be like "Oh, so you're always hard? Well I can be hard too.". This enables hard mode. If you name yourself "hardcore", you get hard mode + no ability to save until the final boss fight. Check points will say "It's just not enough to fill you with determination.".
* If you name yourself kid, boy/girl, hehehe, eek, or various other things, characters will call you Rollie instead. At first, the first character will say that "Rollie" sounds like a good idea, meaning your actual name just magically came to mind. Your character will then later explain that Rollie is actually her original name, and the character will be reasonably surprised.
* If you name yourself "Cake"/"Pie", the first character will try to kill you early on so they can cook you into a cake/pie and it will be a harder battle than normal.

There is actually an ending where you can bring Asriel back to life. It is super duper ultra secret and requires you to do some pokemon "Mew behind the truck" type stuff. But once you do, you get the "Final ending". Simon will sacrifice himself to bring Asriel back. He will says it is okay because his spirit has lots of things to do in the next life. Just like how Asriel has so much left to do in his third life. The theme of the game is how there is always more to life than it seems, and people deserve second chances so they can see more. Even the darkness deserves a second chance. There is an ending where you become friends with it and make the darkness good. It's called the "Brightest darkness ending".

Like checking to see if assets are loaded when a run/reset is started.
